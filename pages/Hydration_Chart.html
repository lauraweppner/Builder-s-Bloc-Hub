This chart is a guide only. Some foods, medications and vitamin supplements can change urine color even when you are hydrated. If you are worried, talk to a doctor.

Signs you are getting dehydrated

  • Thirst or a dry mouth
  • Headache
  • Dizziness or light-headedness
  • Muscle cramps
  • Feeling tired or weak
  • Urinating less than usual
  • Dark urine

Preventing dehydration on the job site

Drink before you are thirsty. In the heat, drink 1 cup (8 oz) of water every 15 to 20 minutes. Small amounts often work better than a lot at once.
Do not overdo it. Stay under 6 cups (48 oz) in one hour.
Start the day hydrated. Drink water before your shift, and keep drinking after you get home.
Replace electrolytes on long, sweaty days. When you are sweating for several hours, add a sports drink with balanced electrolytes, and eat your regular meals.
Skip what dries you out. Avoid alcohol and drinks that are high in caffeine or sugar, including energy drinks.
Keep cool water close. Have water at the work area, not back at the truck, so it is easy to grab.
Take breaks in the shade. Rest somewhere cool, and take breaks more often as the heat goes up.
Ease into the heat. New workers and anyone coming back from time off need a week or two to build up heat tolerance.
Watch out for each other. If a coworker seems confused, stops sweating or cannot keep fluids down, get help. See the First Aid Quick Reference.
Cold weather counts too. You still lose water in winter gear, and you feel less thirsty. Keep drinking year round.
