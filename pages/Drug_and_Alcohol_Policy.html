Drug and Alcohol Policy

Builder's Bloc · Effective Date: 11/20/2026 | Replaces the prior 2023 Drug and Alcohol Policy

The short version

  • Show up fit to work. No drugs or alcohol on job sites, in company vehicles or equipment, or during work time, including breaks.
  • Alcohol at 0.04% or more at work is a violation.
  • Marijuana is not allowed at work, even with a medical card. All field jobs are safety-sensitive, so a positive marijuana test is a violation. CBD and delta-8 are not an excuse.
  • You may be tested before hire, at random, after an accident, on reasonable suspicion, and when you return to work.
  • Refusing a test counts the same as a positive. A verified positive or a refusal means discharge.
  • If a medication could affect your safety, tell your supervisor or HR about the work restriction before you start work. You don't have to say what the condition is.
  • Always report injuries. Testing after an accident is never used to punish you for reporting.
  • Breaking this policy can cut or end your workers' comp benefits under Missouri law.

1. Purpose

The employees of Builder's Bloc are its most valuable resource. The Company is committed to providing a safe workplace and to maintaining programs and procedures that promote safety and health. The use and abuse of drugs and alcohol can seriously affect employees' health, threaten their own safety and the safety of others, and impair job performance.

The purpose of this policy is to keep our job sites, offices, vehicles, and equipment free of drugs and alcohol and to deter the misuse of substances that create a threat to the safety and health of employees and others. All employees are expected to report for work fit to perform their duties safely.

2. Who This Policy Covers

  • This policy applies to all Company employees and to job applicants who have received a conditional offer of employment.

  • Union employees. Field employees are also covered by their collective bargaining agreement and any substance abuse program run by their union. Where a collective bargaining agreement sets testing or rehabilitation procedures that meet or exceed the requirements of this policy, those procedures will be followed.

  • DOT-regulated drivers. Employees who hold a commercial driver's license (CDL) and perform safety-sensitive driving functions are also subject to U.S. Department of Transportation drug and alcohol testing rules (49 CFR Parts 40 and 382) and the Company's separate DOT testing policy. Where DOT rules apply, they control.

  • Safety-sensitive positions. All field positions, including all trades on Company job sites, equipment operators, and anyone who drives a Company vehicle, are designated safety-sensitive because impairment in these jobs could endanger the employee, co-workers, or the public.

3. Definitions

  • Illegal drugs means unprescribed marijuana (as described in Section 6), cocaine, opiates and opioids (including fentanyl), amphetamines and methamphetamine, phencyclidine (PCP), and all other controlled substances as defined in 21 U.S.C. § 802, when used without a valid prescription. It also includes any drug legally obtained by prescription but used other than as prescribed.

  • Company premises and work time means Company offices, job sites, yards, Company vehicles and equipment (including when used off-hours), and any time an employee is on duty or performing Company business, including breaks and meal periods taken on a job site.

  • Verified positive means a test result that has been confirmed by a certified laboratory and reviewed and verified by a Medical Review Officer (MRO).

  • Refusal to test has the meaning given in Section 11.

4. Prohibited Conduct

The following are prohibited:

  • Using, possessing, selling, transferring, manufacturing, distributing, or dispensing illegal drugs on Company premises or during work time.

  • Reporting to work or working with illegal drugs in one's system at or above the cutoff levels described in Section 9.

  • Consuming alcohol in any quantity on Company premises, during work time, or in a Company vehicle at any time, except as allowed in Section 5.

  • Being present on Company premises or during work time, or riding as a driver or passenger in a Company vehicle, with a blood or breath alcohol concentration of 0.04% or more.

  • Using prescription or over-the-counter medication in a way that impairs the employee's ability to work safely.

  • Refusing a test, or tampering with, adulterating, or substituting a specimen.

5. Alcohol

The Company recognizes that alcohol may be legally obtained and used, and that employees may decide whether to drink on their own time, so long as job safety and job performance are not affected. Improper use of alcohol that affects job safety or efficiency is not acceptable.

Moderate use of alcohol is permitted only at Company-sponsored or management-approved events held away from job sites. Employees may not drive a Company vehicle, operate Company equipment, or return to work after drinking at such an event.

6. Marijuana, CBD, and Hemp Products

Missouri law permits the medical and adult (recreational) use of marijuana. Under federal law, adult-use marijuana remains a Schedule I controlled substance; as of April 2026, marijuana covered by a state medical marijuana license and FDA-approved marijuana products are Schedule III. Neither state legalization nor federal rescheduling requires the Company to allow marijuana use, possession, or impairment at work.

  • Using, possessing, or being under the influence of marijuana on Company premises or during work time is prohibited for all employees, including medical marijuana cardholders.

  • Safety-sensitive positions: A verified positive test for marijuana is a violation of this policy, whether or not the employee holds a medical marijuana identification card, because marijuana use in these positions affects the safety of the employee and others.

  • Non-safety-sensitive positions: The Company will not take action against an employee or applicant who holds a valid Missouri medical marijuana identification card based solely on the person's status as a cardholder, lawful use off Company premises during non-working hours, or a positive test for marijuana, unless the person used, possessed, or was under the influence of marijuana on Company premises or during work time, or the use affects the person's ability to perform the job or the safety of others.

  • Use of CBD, hemp-derived THC (such as delta-8 or delta-9 hemp products), or similar products is not an acceptable explanation for a positive marijuana test. Employees use these products at their own risk.

7. Prescription and Over-the-Counter Medications

Employees may use prescription medication as prescribed and over-the-counter medication as directed. Employees taking medication should ask their doctor or pharmacist whether it could affect their safety or ability to do their job. If it could, the employee must tell their supervisor or Human Resources about any resulting work restriction before starting work. Employees do not need to disclose their medical condition, only the restriction. The Company will make reasonable efforts to accommodate the restriction as required by law.

8. When Testing May Occur

The Company may require testing in the following situations:

  • Pre-employment: Job offers may be conditioned on a negative drug test. Applicants will be told about the testing requirement before they are tested.

  • Random: Employees in safety-sensitive positions may be selected for unannounced random testing. Selection is made by a neutral, random method, and each eligible employee has an equal chance of being selected each time.

  • Post-accident: After a workplace accident, injury, or near-miss involving injury, equipment or property damage, or a vehicle, the Company may test every employee whose conduct could have contributed to the incident, whether or not that employee was injured. Testing will be done as soon as possible and no later than 24 hours after the accident.

  • Reasonable suspicion: When a supervisor observes specific signs that an employee may be impaired, such as odor, speech, coordination, behavior, or performance. Observations will be documented in writing.

  • Return-to-duty and follow-up: As described in Section 14.

  • Other required testing: As required by federal, state, or local law, a collective bargaining agreement, or a project owner or customer contract.

Post-accident testing is used to understand what caused an incident. It is never used to discourage or punish employees for reporting injuries or illnesses. All employees must report work-related injuries and illnesses promptly, and the Company will not retaliate against anyone for doing so.

9. Testing Procedures

  • Employees and applicants required to test must sign a written consent and provide urine, oral fluid (saliva), blood, or breath samples as requested.

  • The Company pays for all required testing. Employees will be driven to the collection site and will not be allowed to drive themselves when testing is for reasonable suspicion or after an accident.

  • Specimens are collected and handled using U.S. Department of Transportation chain-of-custody procedures (49 CFR Part 40).

  • All testing is done by a laboratory certified by the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services (HHS) or meeting equivalent standards. Every positive screen is confirmed by mass spectrometry using generally accepted medical or forensic testing procedures. Drug cutoff levels follow the most recent HHS Mandatory Guidelines for Federal Workplace Drug Testing Programs.

  • A Medical Review Officer (MRO) reviews every confirmed positive before it is reported to the Company. The employee will have the opportunity to tell the MRO about any valid prescription or other medical explanation.

10. Right to a Retest

An employee or applicant who receives a verified positive result may request that the original sample (split specimen) be retested by a separate, unrelated certified laboratory using a confirmation method that meets the standards in Section 9. The request must be made to the MRO or Human Resources within 72 hours of being notified of the result.

  • If the retest confirms the original result, the employee pays the cost of the retest.

  • If the retest does not confirm the original result, the Company pays the cost, the test is treated as negative, and any action taken because of it will be reversed.

  • DOT-regulated drivers must request a split specimen test directly from the MRO within 72 hours, as required by 49 CFR § 40.171.

11. Refusal to Test

A refusal to test is treated the same as a verified positive result. A refusal includes:

  • Refusing to take a test, refusing to sign the consent form, or failing to report to the collection site when directed.

  • Leaving the collection site before the test is completed.

  • Failing to provide a sufficient specimen without a valid medical explanation.

  • Adulterating, substituting, diluting, or otherwise tampering with a specimen, or failing to cooperate with the testing process.

12. Consequences of a Violation

  • A verified positive drug test, an alcohol test of 0.04% or more, a refusal to test, or the use, possession, or sale of illegal drugs on Company premises or during work time will result in discharge. For applicants, it will result in the job offer being withdrawn.

  • Other violations of this policy will result in discipline up to and including discharge.

  • An employee who is tested for reasonable suspicion or after an accident may be removed from work until results are received.

  • Any drugs found on Company premises that are suspected of being illegal will be turned over to law enforcement. The Company may notify law enforcement of the illegal use, sale, or possession of drugs at work.

13. Effect on Workers' Compensation

Under Missouri workers' compensation law (RSMo § 287.120.6):

  • If an employee is injured while violating this policy, or while using alcohol or nonprescribed controlled drugs at work, workers' compensation benefits may be reduced by 50%.

  • If the use of alcohol or nonprescribed controlled drugs is the proximate cause of the injury, benefits may be forfeited.

  • An employee's refusal to take a drug or alcohol test after an injury results in forfeiture of benefits, because this policy authorizes post-injury testing.

  • A positive test for a nonprescribed controlled drug creates a rebuttable presumption that the drug was in the employee's system at the time of the injury and that the injury was sustained in connection with its use, if: (a) the initial test was given within 24 hours of the accident or injury; (b) the employee was notified of the results within 14 calendar days after the insurer received actual notice of the confirmed results; (c) the employee was given the opportunity to retest the original sample; and (d) the testing was confirmed by mass spectrometry using generally accepted medical or forensic testing procedures.

14. Rehabilitation and Return to Work

  • Union employees who test positive should contact their union about its rehabilitation program.

  • Non-union employees may enter a rehabilitation program of their choice. The Company's group health insurance may cover part of the cost. The employee is responsible for any remaining cost.

  • DOT-regulated drivers must complete the Substance Abuse Professional (SAP) process required by 49 CFR Part 40 before returning to safety-sensitive driving duties.

  • An employee who successfully completes a rehabilitation program and passes a return-to-duty test will be eligible for re-employment with the Company.

  • Re-employed employees, union and non-union, are subject to unannounced follow-up testing at any time for the remainder of their employment.

  • A re-employed employee who later tests positive for drugs or alcohol at any time, or refuses a test, will be discharged and will not be eligible for rehire.

15. Confidentiality

Test results and related medical information are confidential. They are kept separate from personnel files and shared only with those who need to know, such as Human Resources, the MRO, the employee's union where required by the collective bargaining agreement, the Company's workers' compensation insurer, or as required by law.

16. General Provisions

  • If any federal, state, or local law or regulation limits or prohibits any part of this policy, that part will be ineffective in that jurisdiction only, and the rest of the policy will remain in effect.

  • The Company may revise this policy at any time. Employees will be notified of changes in writing.

  • This policy does not create a contract of employment.
