Severe Weather

🏠 Home

No job is worth getting hurt over weather. If you see lightning, hear thunder or a warning is issued, stop work, take shelter and tell your superintendent.

⚡Lightning

When thunder roars, go indoors. If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck.

Do

  • Stop work and get off roofs, ladders, scaffolds and lifts.
  • Go to a fully enclosed building with wiring and plumbing, or a hard-topped vehicle with the windows up.
  • Stay there until 30 minutes after the last thunder.

Not safe

  • A house that is still being framed, or any open structure
  • Sheds, tents, porches and portable toilets
  • Under trees, or near water and metal
  • Lying flat on the ground

If someone is struck

  • Call 911. They are safe to touch.
  • If they are not breathing or have no pulse, start CPR.
  • Then call Laura Weppner-Herndon at 314-581-6841.

🌪️Tornado

Watch means be ready and know where you would go. Warning means take shelter now.

Do

  • Get inside a sturdy, finished building right away.
  • Go to the basement, or an inside room on the lowest floor, away from windows.
  • Crouch low and cover your head and neck.

Not safe

  • Vehicles, job trailers, sheds and portable toilets
  • A house that is still being framed
  • Under a highway overpass

If you cannot reach a building

  • Drive to the nearest sturdy shelter if there is time.
  • If not, stay buckled in the vehicle with your head down and covered, or get to a low spot like a ditch and cover your head.

💨High Wind

Your superintendent makes the call to stop work at heights.

Do

  • Stop work on roofs and trusses, and get off ladders and scaffolds.
  • Stop carrying sheathing and other sheet goods. They act like sails.
  • Tie down or lay flat loose material, and make sure walls and trusses are braced before you leave them.

Watch for

  • Flying debris and falling material
  • Unbraced walls and trusses
  • Ladders and scaffolds shifting

🌧️Heavy Rain and Flash Flooding

Turn around, do not drown.

Do

  • Stay out of trenches and excavations. They can fill or cave in.
  • Never walk or drive through moving water.
  • Move vehicles, tools and materials out of low areas if there is time.

Remember

  • 6 inches of moving water can knock you off your feet.
  • 12 inches can carry away a car.
  • Wet decks, ladders and mud are slip hazards after the rain stops.

❄️Ice and Snow

Clear it before you work on it.

Do

  • Clear ice and snow from decks, ladders, scaffolds and walkways before use.
  • Watch your footing getting in and out of vehicles and equipment.
  • Dress for the cold. See Heat and Cold Illness.

After the storm: check the site for downed power lines, damaged bracing, standing water and loose material before going back to work. If anyone is hurt, follow Someone Got Hurt: What To Do.
