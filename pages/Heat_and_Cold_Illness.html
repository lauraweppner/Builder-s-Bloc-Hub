Heat and Cold Illness

🏠 Home

Know the signs and act early. Watch your coworkers, because people often do not notice it in themselves.

☀️ Heat

Heat StrokeCALL 911

Signs

  • Confusion, slurred speech or acting strangely
  • Passing out or a seizure
  • Hot, dry skin or heavy sweating
  • Very high body temperature

What to do

  • Call 911 right away. This can kill.
  • Move to shade and take off outer clothing.
  • Cool fast: ice or cold water on the skin, cold wet cloths on the head, neck, armpits and groin.
  • Fan air over them. Stay with them until help arrives.
  • Then call Laura Weppner-Herndon at 314-581-6841.

Heat ExhaustionNEEDS MEDICAL CARE

Signs

  • Headache, dizziness or weakness
  • Nausea
  • Heavy sweating and strong thirst
  • Irritable
  • Urinating less than usual

What to do

  • Get them out of the heat and remove extra clothing.
  • Give frequent sips of cool water.
  • Cool the head, face and neck with cold water or cold cloths.
  • This needs a medical evaluation. Call Laura Weppner-Herndon at 314-581-6841 so she can direct care.
  • If they get confused, pass out or get worse, call 911.

Muscle Breakdown (Rhabdo)NEEDS MEDICAL CARE

Signs

  • Muscle cramps or pain worse than expected
  • Dark, tea or cola colored urine
  • Weak, cannot finish normal tasks

What to do

  • Stop work right away.
  • Drink water.
  • This needs medical care the same day. Call Laura Weppner-Herndon at 314-581-6841 so she can direct care.
  • See the Hydration Chart for urine color.

Heat Cramps

Signs

  • Muscle cramps, pain or spasms in the stomach, arms or legs

What to do

  • Drink water and have a snack or a sports drink every 15 to 20 minutes.
  • Do not take salt tablets.
  • Call Laura Weppner-Herndon if cramps last more than an hour, or the worker has heart problems or is on a low-salt diet.

Fainting or Dizziness

Signs

  • Brief fainting, dizziness or light-headedness after standing a long time or getting up fast

What to do

  • Sit or lie down somewhere cool.
  • Slowly drink water or a sports drink.
  • Any time someone passes out, call Laura Weppner-Herndon at 314-581-6841.

Heat Rash

Signs

  • Clusters of red pimples or small blisters on the neck, chest, groin or elbow creases

What to do

  • Work somewhere cooler and less humid if possible.
  • Keep the rash dry. Powder helps; do not use ointments or creams.

Prevention: drink 1 cup of water every 15 to 20 minutes in the heat, take breaks in the shade, and ease new workers into hot work. See the Hydration Chart.

❄️ Cold

HypothermiaCALL 911

Signs

  • Early: shivering, tired, clumsy, confused
  • Late: shivering stops, blue skin, slow pulse and breathing, passing out

What to do

  • Call 911 for confusion or any late sign.
  • Move to a warm place and take off wet clothing.
  • Warm the chest, neck, head and groin first with blankets or body heat.
  • Give warm drinks if they are awake. No alcohol.
  • If there is no pulse, start CPR.
  • Then call Laura Weppner-Herndon at 314-581-6841.

FrostbiteNEEDS MEDICAL CARE

Signs

  • Numbness, tingling or stinging
  • Aching
  • Bluish or pale, waxy skin, usually fingers, toes, nose or ears

What to do

  • Get into a warm place. Do not walk on frostbitten feet or toes.
  • Soak the area in warm (not hot) water, or warm it with body heat.
  • Do not rub or massage it.
  • Do not use a heating pad, heat lamp, stove or heater. Numb skin burns easily.
  • Call Laura Weppner-Herndon at 314-581-6841 so she can direct care.

Trench Foot (wet, cold feet)

Signs

  • Red skin, numbness or tingling pain
  • Swelling, leg cramps
  • Blisters

What to do

  • Take off wet boots and socks.
  • Dry the feet.
  • Stay off them.
  • Call Laura Weppner-Herndon at 314-581-6841 so she can direct care.

Prevention: dress in layers, keep a dry change of socks and gloves, stay dry, and warm up on breaks. Wind and wet clothing make cold far more dangerous.
